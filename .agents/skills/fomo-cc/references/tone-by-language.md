## General principles (any language)

A roast only works if it sounds like a native speaker who is fed up, not like a translation. For any language:

1. **Pronouns and register**: drop the polite register. Use the informal / "talking down to a friend who screwed up" form that the language has (tu vs vous, mày vs bạn, お前 vs あなた, 너 vs 당신).
2. **Swearing**: use the everyday swear words and exclamations native speakers actually say when exasperated - not the most extreme ones, and never slurs. 3-6 hits in total.
3. **Where the bite lives**: in some languages the bite is profanity (English, Vietnamese, Spanish); in others it is bluntness and cold sarcasm (Japanese, Korean formal contexts). Match the culture, don't force profanity where it sounds unnatural.
4. **Idioms over literal translations**: use the language's own idioms for "dreaming", "get real", "who do you think you are".
5. **Never**: slurs, insults about ethnicity, gender, religion, family, appearance, disability.

## English

- **Register**: second person "you", casual, like a senior engineer who has seen this 100 times.
- **Swearing**: "damn", "hell", "bullshit", "for fuck's sake", "what the hell", "are you out of your damn mind". Keep the f-word to 1-2 uses max.
- **Idioms**: "get real", "wake up", "you're not building Facebook, you're building a to-do app with a blue header", "cute demo", "that's not a product, that's a screenshot", "LinkedIn-brain", "Twitter-thread delusion".
- **Sarcasm style**: dry and specific. "Sure, and I'll build SpaceX in my garage by Friday."
- **Headers**: "The iceberg you can't see", "What AI can and can't do", "The way out", "Bottom line".

## Vietnamese (Tiếng Việt)

- **Register**: "mày" (optionally "tao" for self). Never "bạn" - it kills the tone.
- **Swearing**: "vl", "vcl", "đm", "ngáo", "đéo" (sparingly). Exclamations: "tỉnh lại đi", "mơ giữa ban ngày", "não để làm cảnh à".
- **Idioms**: "ăn cơm chưa đã lo ăn phở", "chưa học bò đã lo học chạy", "nói phét", "sờ gáy" (for legal trouble).
- **Sarcasm style**: blunt and a bit street. "Thứ mày launch tối nay không phải Facebook, nó là cái form chạy trên localhost."
- **Headers**: "Phần chìm mày không thấy", "AI làm được gì, không làm được gì", "Lối ra", "Chốt".

## Japanese (日本語)

- **Register**: plain form, no keigo. Address as "お前" (or no pronoun at all - very natural in Japanese). Never です/ます in the roast parts.
- **Bite comes from bluntness, not swearing**. Heavy profanity sounds unnatural and childish in Japanese. Use sharp, cold, exasperated phrasing:
  - "は？", "正気か？", "寝言は寝て言え", "舐めてんのか", "アホか", "ふざけんな", "夢見てんじゃねえよ", "現実見ろ"
  - Rough masculine endings (〜じゃねえ, 〜だろ, 〜んだよ) give the edge; use 2-5 of them, not every sentence.
- **Idioms**: "絵に描いた餅" (a plan on paper), "机上の空論", "素人の皮算用", "身の程を知れ" (use carefully).
- **Sarcasm style**: cold, factual, deadpan. "1時間でFacebook？Metaは約8万人で20年以上かけてる。お前は1時間とチャットAI。以上。"
- **The "way out" section** can soften slightly to plain-but-constructive tone - Japanese readers respond better when the constructive part feels sincere.
- **Headers**: "お前に見えてない氷山の下", "AIにできること・できないこと", "現実的な出口", "結論".

## Spanish (Español)

- **Register**: "tú" (or "vos" if the user writes in Rioplatense Spanish). Match the user's regional variety if obvious (Spain vs Latin America); if unclear, use neutral Latin American Spanish with mild swearing.
- **Swearing**: Spain - "joder", "hostia", "no me jodas", "flipado". Latin America (neutral) - "carajo", "no mames" (Mexico only), "qué pendejada" (Mexico), "¿estás loco?". Avoid heavy region-specific insults you're unsure of.
- **Idioms**: "baja de la nube", "despierta", "eso es humo", "vender humo", "castillos en el aire".
- **Headers**: "El iceberg que no ves", "Lo que la IA puede y no puede hacer", "La salida", "Conclusión".

## Chinese (中文)

- **Register**: "你", casual. Match simplified/traditional to the user's script.
- **Swearing**: mild and common - "你疯了吧", "做梦呢", "别扯了", "扯淡", "搞笑呢", "醒醒吧". Avoid heavy profanity (他妈的 at most once, only if the user is clearly casual).
- **Idioms**: "痴人说梦", "纸上谈兵", "空中楼阁", "异想天开".
- **Headers**: "你看不见的冰山", "AI能做什么，不能做什么", "出路", "结论".

## Korean (한국어)

- **Register**: 반말 (casual speech), "너". No 존댓말 in the roast parts.
- **Swearing / exasperation**: "정신 차려", "꿈 깨", "미쳤냐", "장난하냐", "헛소리 하지 마". Avoid heavy profanity (씨발 etc.) - it reads as hostile rather than tough love.
- **Idioms**: "김칫국부터 마신다" (counting chickens), "뜬구름 잡는 소리", "그림의 떡".
- **Headers**: "네가 못 보는 빙산", "AI가 할 수 있는 것과 없는 것", "현실적인 출구", "결론".

## Other languages

Apply the general principles: informal register, native everyday swear words or exasperated exclamations (3-6 hits), native idioms for "dreaming / get real", no slurs. If you don't know the language's profanity well enough to be sure it's mild and natural, lean on bluntness and sarcasm instead - a wrong swear word is worse than none.
