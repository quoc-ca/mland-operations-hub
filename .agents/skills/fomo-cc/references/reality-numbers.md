# Reality numbers to crush the delusion

Figures checked in September 2026 from annual filings (10-K, 20-F) and press. Use orders of magnitude when speaking ("nearly 80,000", "over 3 billion") rather than exact figures, since numbers shift quarterly. For products not listed here: use web search if available; otherwise stick to cautious orders of magnitude.

## Social networks

**Facebook / Meta**
- Founded 2004 - 20+ years of operations
- ~79,000 employees (end of 2025)
- Family of Apps: ~4 billion monthly users; Facebook alone ~3 billion MAU
- ~15,000 people in content moderation (employees + contractors) - just to review and remove bad content
- Builds its own data centers on multiple continents and designs its own hardware (Open Compute Project)

**X / Twitter**
- Founded 2006. 2007-2010 infamous for the "Fail Whale" - constant outages under load; the backend had to be rewritten from Ruby to the JVM
- ~7,500 employees before Musk's acquisition (Oct 2022)
- After cutting ~80%: ~1,500, later growing back to roughly 2,000-3,000
- Roast angle: even the most brutal cost-cutter in tech had to keep 1,500+ people to stop X from falling over - and you plan to run it alone with AI?
- In 2024 X had to rehire for security and safety after cutting too deep

**Instagram** (the "tiny team" example people misuse)
- Launched Oct 2010, acquired by Facebook Apr 2012 for $1B with 13 employees and ~30M users
- But: nearly 2 years, a team of strong engineers, then scaled on Facebook's infrastructure

**WhatsApp** (another "tiny team" example)
- Founded 2009, acquired by Facebook in 2014 for ~$19B with ~55 employees and ~450M users
- But: 5 years, founders were long-time Yahoo engineers, built on Erlang - a specialist stack for messaging
- Now 3+ billion users
- Roast angle: small teams can build big products - over years and with deep expertise, not with one hour of prompting

## Ride-hailing / delivery

**Uber**
- Founded 2009
- ~34,000 employees (end of 2025)
- 70+ countries, 15,000+ cities - each with its own laws and payment habits
- Hidden part: real-time dispatch, dynamic pricing, maps, driver KYC, insurance, accident handling, fraud, lawsuits

**Grab**
- Founded 2012 (as MyTeksi, Malaysia)
- ~12,000 employees (end of 2025)
- Super app: rides, food, payments, financial services - needs financial licenses in each country

**Gojek** (the "even the big players lose" example)
- Entered Vietnam in 2018 (as GoViet), exited Vietnam on 16 Sep 2024 - Vietnam was under 1% of parent GoTo's GTV. Had already left Thailand in 2021
- Roast angle: a well-funded, experienced super app gave up on this market - your weekend project is going to win it?

## E-commerce

**Shopee / Sea Group**
- Shopee launched 2015
- Sea group has tens of thousands of staff
- Hidden part: logistics, warehouses, payments, counterfeit control, flash sales with millions of concurrent requests, customer service, refunds

**Amazon**
- Founded 1994, 30+ years
- 1.5+ million employees (including fulfillment)

## Streaming

**Netflix**
- Founded 1997, streaming since 2007
- ~16,000 employees (end of 2025)
- Built its own CDN (Open Connect) with servers in close to 1,000 ISP/IXP locations across 100+ countries
- Invented Chaos Engineering because the system is too big to just hope it doesn't break

## General references

- Code is usually a small share of the total effort behind a successful product. Most of it is: finding users, operations, fixing bugs after release, support, legal, marketing
- Most startups die because they build something nobody needs, not because they coded too slowly
- Network effects: a social network without users is an empty database. Code can be cloned, users can't
- Super apps are an outcome, not a starting strategy: Grab started with taxis, Shopee with a marketplace, WeChat with messaging
