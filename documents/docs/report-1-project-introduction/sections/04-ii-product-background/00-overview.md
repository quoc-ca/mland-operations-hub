# II. Product Background {#product-background}

Mland runs ring-making workshops at two branches in Hà Nội and is preparing a third branch in Đà Nẵng. Each branch serves about 20–30 customers per day, including local customers and tourists from the United Kingdom, France, and Germany. Customers come for three reasons: to make their own ring in a workshop, to order a ring made by the shop, or to buy a ready-made ring. At least two customer-service Staff per branch handle all of these requests by phone, Instagram, and WhatsApp.

A typical day for a Staff member starts with unread messages. A tourist asks in English whether four people can join the afternoon session. The Staff member checks a notebook for free seats, quotes the price, sends bank details for the deposit, and waits for a transfer screenshot. Meanwhile, a couple sends photos of a ring they saw online and asks what a similar one would cost. The Staff member forwards the photos to the Manager, who replies hours later; by then the couple has changed the stone. At the session, Staff searches the chat history to see who paid what before collecting the rest.

None of this is recorded in one place. Seats can be double-booked because availability lives in notes and messages. Deposits cannot be matched reliably to bookings, and design ideas, decisions, and quotes are scattered across conversations, so the same questions are asked again. When a Staff member is absent, the context of their customers is lost. The Manager has no reliable view of revenue, attendance, or open requests across branches.

The problem grows with every branch. Opening Đà Nẵng would add another team handling the same messages without shared availability or records. Foreign visitors also need a bilingual self-service path instead of waiting for a reply. Mland therefore needs a single system before the third branch opens.
