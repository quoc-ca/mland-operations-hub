# III. Existing Solutions {#existing-solutions}

The ring-customization workflow proposed for Mland and Domino's “Build Your Own Pizza” feature share the same basic product-design principle: a customer starts with a base product and personalises it by selecting from a controlled set of components. Domino's guides the customer through choices such as size, crust, sauce, and toppings, while Mland's workflow may guide the customer through ring designs, materials, gemstones, shapes, and available components. In both cases, the customer can make decisions step by step instead of describing the entire product informally to an employee, and the selected options can be used to determine whether the product is available and how much it may cost. Domino's also indicates that availability and additional charges can vary by store, which is comparable to Mland's need to consider branch capabilities and maintained component data ([Domino's Build Your Own Pizza menu](https://cache.dominos.com/olo/6.162.0/assets/build/market/KN/_en/pdf/national_menu.pdf); [Domino's ordering site](https://www.dominos.com/en/)).

The important difference is the level of uncertainty. A pizza configuration is usually validated immediately against a predefined menu and production process. A customised ring may begin with an incomplete idea or reference image and can require feasibility analysis, clarification, an estimate, and Staff or Owner review before the customer can proceed. Therefore, Domino's demonstrates the value of guided customization and immediate option-based ordering, while the Mland system extends the same idea into a traceable workflow that connects customization with feasibility, workshop booking, payment, fulfilment, and human decision-making.
